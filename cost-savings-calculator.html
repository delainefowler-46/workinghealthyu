Why the numbers matter more than most employers realize

A single musculoskeletal injury costs an average of $40,000 or more in combined direct and indirect costs. That's one claim. Most facilities with 100 or more employees see several every year.

The indirect costs are the part that usually surprises people. Lost productivity, retraining time, administrative overhead, and the impact on your Experience Modification Rate — these rarely show up on the same line as the workers comp claim, but they compound year after year.

What goes into this estimate

Your industry's musculoskeletal injury rate per 100 employees, based on BLS DART data
A regional cost index based on your state's workers comp environment and wage rates
A 58% cost reduction assumption based on documented early intervention outcomes
A base cost per claim of $40,000, consistent with published industry data

This calculator gives you a directional estimate, not a guarantee. Your actual results depend on program design, implementation quality, and how engaged your workforce is. The best way to get a number specific to your facility is to schedule a call — we will walk through your actual claims history and build a realistic projection from there.

Methodology note: Industry injury rates are sourced from BLS employer-reported injury and illness data (2023-2024). Regional cost indices reflect published workers compensation cost variation by state. The 58% cost reduction assumption reflects outcomes documented in peer-reviewed early intervention program research. Individual results will vary.
